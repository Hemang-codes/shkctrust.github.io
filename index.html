Our story

Rooted in devotion, working in the open

Shree Hari Krishna Charitable Trust was founded by a small group of devotees in Vrindavan who began by cooking for pilgrims outside the temple gates. What started as a single stove has grown into a registered trust running kitchens, schools, a goshala and mobile health camps across the region — but the founding instinct hasn't changed: see who is hungry, who is unwell, who is without means, and go to them first.

We are guided by the Vaishnava principle of seva — service offered without expectation of return — and we run every program with the same discipline we'd want from any institution asking for public trust: audited accounts, named trustees and a door that's open to anyone who wants to see the kitchen, the classroom or the goshala for themselves.

Every rupee received is logged against a program, not a general fund, so a donor supporting the school can see it reach the school.

Where the offering goes

Six forms of seva, one purpose

Each program is run by a small resident team, not a rotating volunteer roster, so the same faces are there tomorrow that were there today.

Annadaan — free kitchens

Three community kitchens cook and serve fresh vegetarian meals daily to pilgrims, daily-wage workers and families who would otherwise go without.

Education support

A free school and after-hours tuition centre for children from families who cannot afford fees, plus uniforms, books and a midday meal.

Goshala — cow protection

A sanctuary for aged, injured and abandoned cows, run on organic fodder cultivation and traditional gaushala care practices.

Health camps

Monthly free medical camps with visiting doctors offering check-ups, medicines and referrals to nearby villages with no clinic of their own.

Spiritual care

Daily aarti, free distribution of the Bhagavad Gita, and evening satsang open to anyone who wants a quiet hour of chanting and reflection.

Shelter & relief

Emergency relief kits and temporary shelter for families displaced by flood or fire, coordinated with local district authorities.

Join us

Festivals open to all, regardless of faith

Our festival days double as our largest seva days — every celebration ends with a free meal for whoever has come.

August

Janmashtami

An all-night vigil marking Krishna's birth, with kirtan, a midnight abhishek and a dawn feast for the whole town.

September

Radhastami

A day honouring Srimati Radharani, with flower decoration of the deities and readings from the Gita for visiting families.

November

Annakut

A mountain of home-cooked offerings prepared by the kitchen team and shared with every visitor who walks through the gate.
